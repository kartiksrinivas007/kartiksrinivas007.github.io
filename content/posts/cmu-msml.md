> **Note:** This is a deliberately opinionated account, based on my experience in the program.

After completing my 1.5-year master's degree at CMU MLD, I wanted to reflect on what CMU offers through its [MSML program](https://ml.cmu.edu/academics/machine-learning-masters-curriculum). I have taken and TA'd several of these courses, and I have also served on the program's advisory and admissions committees. This is my perspective on the program's strengths, weaknesses, and surprises.

## What makes a good course?

For me, the course material is the most important part of a program. A good course should have the following qualities:

1. The material is interesting, relevant, and new to the learner.
2. The material is presented clearly through well-designed slides or lecture notes.
3. The assignments are thoughtfully designed, challenging, and genuinely instructive.
4. The exams assess understanding of the material and are reasonably aligned with the lectures.
5. The lecturer is well rehearsed and clear in what they say. A great lecture is almost like watching a movie: engaging, deliberate, and easy to follow.

## Semester 1

*Courses I considered outstanding are marked with a ⭐.*

### 10-715: Introduction to Machine Learning

- **Notes and slides:** Weak.
- **Assignments:** Very good, especially the TA-designed assignments.
- **Lectures:** Weak; the lack of slides made the course harder to follow.
- **Exams:** Weakly aligned with the lectures.

### ⭐ 36-705: Intermediate Statistics

- **Notes and assignments:** Outstanding; they have clearly been refined over many years.
- **Lecturer:** Larry Wasserman was outstanding.
- **Exams:** Excellent.

## Semester 2

### ⭐ 10-708: Probabilistic Graphical Models

- **Notes and assignments:** Outstanding.
- **Lecturers:** Andrej Risteski and Albert Gu were very good.
- **Exams:** Outstanding.

### 36-709: Advanced Statistical Theory

- **Notes:** Drawn from *Asymptotic Statistics*.
- **Lecturer:** Yandi Shen was a solid lecturer.
- **Exams:** Good.

### ⭐ 15-513: Computer Systems

- **Notes and lectures:** Outstanding. Dave Andersen is an exceptional lecturer.
- **Assignments:** Excellent and demanding, including building your own `malloc` implementation and file system.
- **Exams:** Difficult finals, in a good way.

### ⭐ 15-645: Database Systems

This was the best course I took at CMU.

- **Lectures:** Jignesh Patel's lectures were exceptional.
- **Assignments:** Extremely hard and rewarding; you build your own database system in C++.
- **Materials and exams:** Outstanding slides and notes, with decent exams.

### 15-642: ML Systems

- **Notes:** Decent.
- **Assignments:** Good; you build your own automatic-differentiation engine.
- **Assessment:** Project-based, with no exams, which made the course enjoyable.
- **Lectures:** The lectures can take some getting used to, but the course is worthwhile overall.

## Semester 3

### 10-718: Machine Learning in Practice

- **Assignments:** Tedious, with a substantial amount of data-science work.
- **Project:** Restrictive because students are assigned projects rather than choosing them.
- **Lectures:** Light on technical material.
- **Lecturer:** Rayid Ghani is a clear and effective speaker.

### ⭐ 10-703: Deep Reinforcement Learning and Control

- **Assignments, notes, and lectures:** Wonderful. Katerina's teaching was excellent.
- **Exams:** Good.
- **Culture:** The course had a strong scribing culture, which I appreciated.

## TA'ing courses

I TA'd for **10-725** and **10-708**. In my experience, the work was not especially demanding and was easily manageable alongside a regular course load.

**Perks:**

- You get paid.
- You get really cool course merchandise.

## What I would have done differently

If I were doing the program again, I would take **15-513: Computer Systems** in the summer before arriving. That would let me take more advanced systems courses, such as **15-645: Database Systems**, in my first semester. I would also make room for Parallel Computing and Operating Systems—both notoriously difficult and extremely rewarding courses.

I discovered too late that CMU's systems courses are among the best in the world, and I was only able to take three of them. I would plan more deliberately from the start to take advantage of courses across CMU and to maximize how much I could learn.

### A controversial take on research advising

My controversial take is that advisors often want master's students to help PhD students execute the PhD students' ideas, rather than giving master's students the space to develop their own. That is understandable: an advisor's primary responsibility is to their PhD student, and they may not yet trust a master's student with a core idea.

There are exceptions. If you demonstrate that you are a strong student, you may be able to convince an advisor to invest more deeply in you or even convert into a PhD track. In my view, though, that is relatively rare. If I were pursuing a master's degree again, I would focus first on maximizing my learning through coursework and projects.
